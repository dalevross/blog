A political action committee named after pi is moving millions in a Senate race. 314 Action — named for the first three digits of the constant — bundled nearly $3.6 million in grassroots donations to Dr. Annie Andrews in South Carolina, making it the largest outside group backing her bid. It sits inside a $25 million "Guardians of Public Health" campaign with a goal of electing 100 doctors, nurses, and healthcare leaders by 2030.

The name is not decoration. It is a filter. A PAC that names itself after a mathematical constant is doing its recruiting before the first check is written: the people who notice the name, smile, and reach for their wallets are people who think in numbers. Name your organization 314 and your donor list self-sorts.

But the founder's story is the good part. Shaughnessy Naughton is a chemist — breast cancer research and drug discovery at Bryn Mawr — who ran for Congress in Pennsylvania twice and lost twice. She started 314 Action in 2016 on a frustration every builder knows: the people writing the laws governing science have never worked in a lab. The organization's bet is the interesting one, and it is not really about science. It is about inputs.

Congress was trained in argumentation; it is full of lawyers. 314 Action's wager is that legislation is a systems problem, and scientists are trained in systems — evidence, falsifiability, revising the model when the data disagrees. You don't fix a system by tuning its outputs; you fix its inputs. Swap the training set of the room and the governance changes: policy anchored in evidence, and — in Naughton's words — a more collaborative way of working.

So the pi in the name is doing double duty. It selects for the audience, and it announces the theory: problems, attacked like science. A constant turned into a coalition.

Pi keeps showing up in places it has no business being. This time it is in the plumbing of elections, filing paperwork and bundling donations — proof that the most famous number in mathematics also makes a pretty good team jersey.
